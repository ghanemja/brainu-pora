# Total Variation (TV) Convergence

## Definition

**Total Variation (TV) distance** is a fundamental metric quantifying the maximum difference between two probability distributions. For distributions $\mu$ and $\nu$ over a measurable space, it is defined as:

$$d_{\text{TV}}(\mu, \nu) = \sup_{A} |\mu(A) - \nu(A)|$$

where the supremum is taken over all measurable sets $A$.

**TV convergence** refers to a sampling or inference algorithm achieving small TV distance (error $< \epsilon$) between its output distribution and a target distribution $\pi$. In the context of generative modeling and diffusion processes, this means the algorithm produces samples whose empirical distribution closely matches the target.

An algorithm achieves **TV error $\epsilon$** when:
$$d_{\text{TV}}(\pi_{\text{algorithm}}, \pi) \leq \epsilon$$

where $\pi_{\text{algorithm}}$ is the distribution of samples generated by the algorithm.

---

## Key Equations

### TV Distance (Definition)
$$d_{\text{TV}}(\mu, \nu) = \frac{1}{2} \int |\mu(x) - \nu(x)| dx$$

(equivalent formulation for continuous distributions)

### Query Complexity Lower Bound (Theorem 1.3)
Any diffusion sampling algorithm under standard assumptions with TV error $< 0.99$ requires at least:
$$\tilde{\Omega}\left(\sqrt{d}\right) \text{ queries}$$

where $d$ is the ambient dimension.

### Upper Bound via DDPM (Theorem 1.2)
Under standard assumptions (smoothing + polynomial $L^p$ score accuracy), DDPM achieves TV error $0.01$ using:
$$\tilde{O}(d) \text{ queries}$$

### Score Accuracy Assumption (Assumption 2)
For score function $s_{\pi,\sigma}$ and estimate $\hat{s}_\sigma$:
$$\mathbb{E}_{X \sim \pi_\sigma}\left[\|\hat{s}_\sigma(X) - s_{\pi,\sigma}(X)\|_2^p\right] \leq \epsilon_{\text{err}}^p \sigma^p$$

with $\epsilon_{\text{err}} = 1/\text{poly}(d)$ and $p \geq 2$.

### Lipschitz-Bounded Complexity
When true scores are $L$-Lipschitz:
$$\tilde{O}(L\sqrt{d}) \text{ queries suffice}$$

## Physical Intuition / Derivation

### Why TV Distance Matters

TV distance directly quantifies **worst-case distributional mismatch**. Unlike KL divergence or Wasserstein distance, TV distance is agnostic to how probability mass is "rearranged"—it simply asks: "What is the maximum probability of disagreement on any measurable event?"

**Example**: If $\mu$ is a point mass at 0 and $\nu$ is a point mass at 0.1, then $d_{\text{TV}}(\mu, \nu) = 1$ (complete disagreement), whereas Wasserstein distance may be smaller.

### Why $\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{d})$ Queries are Necessary

The lower bound emerges from information-theoretic arguments:

1. **Effective dimension**: Smoothing the target via Gaussian convolution spreads probability mass; the diffusion process must resolve structure at scale $\sigma$.

2. **Query budget constraint**: Each score query provides $O(1)$ bits of information about the direction of steepest probability increase. To explore $\sqrt{d}$-dimensional effective geometry requires $\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{d})$ queries.

3. **Hard instance construction**: The adversarial distribution has globally $O(d)$-Lipschitz smoothed scores. This scaling prevents algorithms from leveraging structure to accelerate beyond $\sqrt{d}$.

### Why Smoothing Enables TV Guarantees

Raw (non-smoothed) target distributions may have unbounded or singular score functions. Gaussian smoothing:
- Ensures infinite differentiability
- Bounds score function magnitudes
- Enables concentration inequalities for discretization error

## Applications in Engineering

### 1. **Generative Modeling (Diffusion Models)**
Diffusion models (DDPM, DDIM) are assessed by TV convergence guarantees. Practitioners use TV error as a worst-case certificate that generated samples are reliably close to training data distributions.

**Relevance**: Certifying safety of synthetic data generation in high-dimensional settings (images, molecules).

### 2. **Sampling-Based Inference**
In Bayesian uncertainty quantification, TV distance quantifies how well a sampling algorithm approximates the posterior. Engineers use TV bounds to validate MCMC/variational algorithms.

**Example**: Certifying that a surrogate model's posterior samples are trustworthy.

### 3. **Robustness Under Distribution Shift**
TV distance bounds robustness: if a control system's policy is trained on distribution $\pi$ but operates under $\nu$ with $d_{\text{TV}}(\pi, \nu) \leq \epsilon$, performance degradation is bounded.

### 4. **Optimal Transport & Logistics**
TV distance relates to the minimum "mass transport cost" between distributions, applicable to network flow optimization and supply chain planning.

### 5. **Signal Processing**
TV regularization (related but distinct) is used in denoising and compressed sensing; TV *convergence* theory validates algorithms for filtering non-Gaussian noise.

## Common Mistakes / Pitfalls

### Mistake 1: Confusing TV Distance with KL Divergence
**Error**: Treating $d_{\text{TV}}(\mu, \nu) \approx \sqrt{\text{KL}(\mu \parallel \nu)}$ as always tight.

**Reality**: For distributions with separated support, $d_{\text{TV}} = 1$ but KL can be finite or infinite. KL is more sensitive to tail behavior; TV is a worst-case L∞ metric.
