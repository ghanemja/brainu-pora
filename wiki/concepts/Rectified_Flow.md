# Rectified Flow

## Definition

**Rectified Flow** is a generative modeling technique that learns to generate data by constructing straight-line trajectories in data space that connect simple noise distributions to complex data distributions. Unlike diffusion models that require iterative refinement through many noisy steps, rectified flow trains neural networks to map data points along optimal transport paths, enabling faster and more direct sampling.

Formally, rectified flow learns a velocity field $\mathbf{v}_\theta(t, \mathbf{x})$ that parameterizes the dynamics:
$$\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = \mathbf{v}_\theta(t, \mathbf{x})$$

such that straight-line paths $\mathbf{x}(t) = (1-t)\mathbf{z} + t\mathbf{x}_0$ (where $\mathbf{z} \sim p_0$ is noise and $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim p_{\text{data}}$ is a data point) are optimal trajectories from the noise distribution $p_0$ to the data distribution $p_{\text{data}}$ over time $t \in [0,1]$.

## Key Equations

### Velocity Field Training Objective

The rectified flow is trained to minimize the L2 distance between the learned velocity and the ground truth transport velocity:

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x}_0}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_\theta(t, (1-t)\mathbf{z} + t\mathbf{x}_0) - (\mathbf{x}_0 - \mathbf{z})\right\|^2\right]$$

where the optimal velocity at any point on the straight path is simply $\mathbf{v}^*(t, \mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x}_0 - \mathbf{z}$, constant along each trajectory.

### Generative Sampling via ODE

Once trained, samples are generated by solving the probability flow ODE:

$$\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = \mathbf{v}_\theta(t, \mathbf{x}), \quad \mathbf{x}(0) \sim p_0$$

using standard numerical ODE solvers (e.g., Euler, RK45), obtaining $\mathbf{x}(1) \sim p_{\text{data}}$.

### Reflow Operation

Rectified flow can be iteratively improved through a "reflow" procedure: sampling from the current model and then training a new model on the learned trajectories, which progressively straightens the paths and improves transport optimality.

## Physical Intuition / Derivation

### Why Straight Lines?

The key insight is **optimal transport theory**: the fastest, most direct way to move probability mass from one distribution to another is along geodesics (straight lines in Euclidean space). By explicitly constructing paths that follow straight lines, rectified flow avoids the inefficiency of diffusion models, which use curved, noisy trajectories requiring many refinement steps.

### Connection to Flow Matching

Rectified flow is related to flow matching approaches but with a crucial distinction: instead of matching arbitrary vector fields, it specifically enforces that trajectories follow the straight line connecting noise and data pairs. This is both more interpretable and more computationally efficient.

### Training Dynamic

During training:
1. Sample noise $\mathbf{z} \sim p_0$ (e.g., standard Gaussian)
2. Sample data $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim p_{\text{data}}$
3. Construct trajectory: $\mathbf{x}(t) = (1-t)\mathbf{z} + t\mathbf{x}_0$
4. The optimal velocity field is simply the displacement vector $\mathbf{x}_0 - \mathbf{z}$
5. Train $\mathbf{v}_\theta$ to predict this displacement

This is far simpler than score-matching in diffusion models, where networks must learn the score function (gradient of log density) at every noise level.

### Why This Works Theoretically

Under the assumption of straight-line transport:
- The velocity field is **deterministic and constant along each path**
- Learning the velocity becomes a **supervised regression problem** rather than density estimation
- Fewer function evaluations needed at test time (often 10-100 steps vs. 1000 for diffusion)

## Applications in Engineering

### 1. **High-Dimensional Data Generation**
- Image synthesis with fewer inference steps than diffusion models
- Faster generation enables real-time or latency-sensitive applications

### 2. **Scientific Computing & Inverse Problems**
- Generating solutions to PDEs by learning mappings in solution space
- Uncertainty quantification in computational models

### 3. **Conditional Generation & Data Transfer**
- Learning transformations between distributions (e.g., style transfer, domain adaptation)
- Optimal transport-based data augmentation

### 4. **Accelerated Sampling**
- Reducing computational cost of generative models through straighter trajectories
- Critical for resource-constrained devices (embedded systems, edge computing)

### 5. **Hybrid Models**
- Combining with neural ODE solvers for controllable generation
- Integration with physics-informed neural networks (PINNs)

## Common Mistakes / Pitfalls

### 1. **Assuming Linear Interpolation is Always Optimal**
Straight lines in Euclidean space are only optimal transport paths when using the $L^2$ metric. For non-Euclidean geometries or different cost functions, the structure may need modification.

### 2. **Ignoring Reflow**
Early rectified flow models can be suboptimal. Many practitioners skip the iterative reflow procedure, which significantly improves path straightness and sampling quality.

### 3. **Confusing with Flow Matching**
Rectified flow is a *specific instantiation* of the flow matching framework—not synonymous with it. Flow matching is more general; rectified flow adds the constraint of straight-line paths.
